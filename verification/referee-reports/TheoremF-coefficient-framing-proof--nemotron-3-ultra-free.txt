## Three Steps I Am Least Sure About (and Why)

1. **Lemma 3(ii) cancellation of S₂^± (line 30)**
   - The argument that Σ_{v<pᵉ/2} v⁻² ≡ ½S₂ (mod pᵉ) relies on pairing v with pᵉ−v and the congruence (pᵉ−v)⁻² ≡ v⁻² (mod pᵉ). This is correct, but the step "Σ_{v<pᵉ/2} v⁻² ≡ ½S₂" is a congruence mod pᵉ, not an equality. Since S₂ ≡ 0 (mod pᵉ) by Lemma 2, the conclusion Σ_{v<pᵉ/2} v⁻² ≡ 0 (mod pᵉ) is valid. However, the proof writes it as an equality ("≡ ½S₂") without explicitly stating the modulus, which could confuse a reader checking the p-adic precision.

2. **Lemma 6 application when τ = r−2 and r=2 (line 57)**
   - When r=2, τ ≤ 0. The case τ=0 gives σ=0. Lemma 6 requires σ ≤ r−2 = 0, which holds. The bound v_p(γ_s) ≥ r−1−τ = 1 is then used. This works, but the condition "σ ≤ r−2" in Lemma 6 is tight: if r=2 and σ=0, the lemma applies; if r=1, the condition is vacuous. The proof implicitly relies on r≥1 (true by definition) and handles r=1 in the first case (τ ≥ r−1). The boundary r=2 is the most delicate and deserves explicit verification, which the computer checks provide.

3. **The j≥3 valuation bound for p=5, j=5 (line 54)**
   - The proof claims v_p(N^j/j!) ≥ 3r using v_p(j!) ≤ (j−1)/4. For p=5, j=5: v_5(5!) = 1. The bound gives 5r − 1 ≥ 3r ⇒ 2r ≥ 1, which holds for r≥1. But the inequality v_p(j!) ≤ (j−1)/(p−1) is Legendre's formula (v_p(j!) = Σ floor(j/p^i) < j/(p−1)). For j=5, p=5: v_5(5!) = 1, while (5−1)/4 = 1. The inequality is tight here. The proof's estimate "v_p(j!) ≤ (j−1)/(p−1) ≤ (j−1)/4 ≤ (j−3)r" is a chain of inequalities; the last step (j−1)/4 ≤ (j−3)r requires r≥1 and j≥4, which holds. It is correct but very loose; a direct computation of v_p(j!) for small j would be more transparent.
