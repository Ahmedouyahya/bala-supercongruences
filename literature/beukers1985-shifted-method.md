## 1. Beukers 1985: what is proved

Beukers writes (p. 141) a_n = Σ_{k=0}^{n} C(n,k)² C(n+k,k) (the ζ(2) numbers, 1, 3, 19, 147, 1251, …) and u_n = Σ_{k=0}^{n} C(n,k)² C(n+k,k)² (the ζ(3) numbers, 1, 5, 73, 1445, 33001, …). Both are treated.

**[quoted, p. 142] THEOREM 1.** "Let m, r ∈ ℕ and p > 3 prime. Then u_{mp^r − 1} ≡ u_{mp^{r−1} − 1} (mod p^{3r}), a_{mp^r − 1} ≡ a_{mp^{r−1} − 1} (mod p^{3r})."

"The proof of these congruences is quite involved and we defer its presentation to Sections 2 and 3. It would be very nice if a more natural proof of Theorem 1 were found instead of the 'brute force method' we employed." **[quoted, p. 142]**

**[quoted, p. 142] THEOREM 2.** "Let m, r ∈ ℕ and let p be any prime. Then u_{mp^r − 1} ≡ u_{mp^{r−1} − 1} (mod p^r), a_{mp^r − 1} ≡ a_{mp^{r−1} − 1} (mod p^r)." Its proof (pp. 142–143) uses three congruences, valid for all m ∈ ℤ, k, r ∈ ℕ:
(i) C(mp^r, k) ≡ 0 (mod p^r) if k ≢ 0 (mod p) ("trivial since C(mp^r,k) = (mp^r/k) C(mp^r − 1, k − 1)");
(ii) C(mp^r, kp) ≡ C(mp^{r−1}, k)(−1)^{k(p−1)} (mod p^r) (from (1 − X)^{mp^r} ≡ (1 − X^p)^{mp^{r−1}} mod p^r);
(iii) C(mp^r − 1, k) ≡ C(mp^{r−1} − 1, [k/p]) (−1)^{k − [k/p]} (mod p^r) (from (1 − X)^{mp^r − 1} ≡ (1 − X^p)^{mp^{r−1} − 1}(1 + X + … + X^{p−1}) mod p^r).
Then, "on noticing that C(n+k,k) = (−1)^k C(−n−1,k)", a_{mp^r − 1} = Σ_{k=0}^{mp^r − 1} C(mp^r − 1, k)² C(−mp^r, k)(−1)^k; the p ∤ k terms are ≡ 0 mod p^r by (i), and (ii), (iii) reduce the p | k terms to a_{mp^{r−1} − 1}.
**Labeling note:** the congruence the task description calls "Lemma 2" is Beukers's congruence **(iii)** above (= Straub's Lemma 5.4, Osburn–Sahu–Straub's Lemma 2.4); Beukers's own **Lemma 2** is the *exact* product identity behind it (§2 below).

Also in §1: a Proposition (p. 143) that c_{mp^r} ≡ c_{mp^{r−1}} (mod p^r) for all m, r, p is equivalent to exp(Σ c_n T^n / n) ∈ ℤ[[T]] (Dwork's lemma / Hazewinkel's functional-equation lemma), hence Theorem 2 says ∫ (Σ a_n t^n) dt and ∫ (Σ u_n t^n) dt are logarithms of formal groups over ℤ isomorphic to G_m; Theorem 3 (p. 145, a consequence of Stienstra–Beukers on the formal Brauer group of elliptic K3 surfaces) is an Atkin–Swinnerton-Dyer-type congruence for the ζ(2) numbers: for p odd, m odd, r ∈ ℕ, a_{(mp^r − 1)/2} − α_p a_{(mp^{r−1} − 1)/2} + (−1)^{(p−1)/2} p² a_{(mp^{r−2} − 1)/2} ≡ 0 (mod p^r), with α_p = 0 if p ≡ 3 (mod 4) and α_p = 4a² − 2p if p = a² + b², a odd (terms with negative index taken as 0); Beukers remarks that numerically (6) seems to hold mod p^{2r} for p ≥ 5, "However, we have no idea to prove this." **[quoted, p. 145]** "The congruences in Theorem 1 are stronger than the ones suggested by formal group theory. For that reason we named them supercongruences." **[quoted, p. 145]** Page 146 records the numerical observations u_{mp^r} ≡ u_{mp^{r−1}} mod p^{3r+1} for (m,p) = (1,5), (3,5), (4,5), (5,7), (2,11) and mod p^{3r+2} for (2,5), (7,5), stated as open.

## 2. The lemmas (§2, pp. 146–151)

Standing notation (p. 146): "p fixed prime ≥ 5; r fixed natural number; [x] largest integer not exceeding x; {x} = x − [x]; Π' or Σ' over k ∈ V: take the product or sum over those values k ∈ V for which p ∤ k; v_p(n) number of prime factors p in n." **[quoted]**

**[quoted, p. 146] LEMMA 1.** "For any l ∈ ℤ we have Σ'_{[λp^{−r}] = l} 1/λ ≡ 0 (mod p^{2r})."
Proof (4 lines): pair λ with (2l+1)p^r − λ inside the block: Σ' 1/λ = ½ Σ' (1/λ + 1/((2l+1)p^r − λ)) ≡ ½ Σ' (1/λ − (1/λ)(1 + (2l+1)p^r/λ + …)) ≡ −(p^r/2)(2l+1) Σ'_{λ=1}^{p^r − 1} 1/λ² ≡ 0 (mod p^{2r}). The final step uses Σ'_{λ<p^r} 1/λ² ≡ 0 (mod p^r) for p ≥ 5, which Beukers does not prove (Straub's Lemma 5.2 does, via scaling by α with α² ≢ 1).
**[inference]** This is the only "Wolstenholme" input in the paper: a block version of H_{p−1} ≡ 0 (mod p²), proved by the classical pairing + Σ 1/λ² argument. No Bernoulli numbers, nothing at the B_{p−3} level, no Glaisher/Jacobsthal refinements appear anywhere in the paper.

**[quoted, pp. 146–147] LEMMA 2.** "For any m ∈ ℤ, k ∈ ℕ we have
(i) C(mp^r − 1, k) = C(mp^{r−1} − 1, [k/p]) Π'_{λ=1}^{k} (mp^r − λ)/λ.
If p | k, then (ii) C(mp^r, k) = C(mp^{r−1}, k/p) Π'_{λ=1}^{k} (mp^r − λ)/λ."
These are *exact identities* (split the product Π_{λ≤k}(mp^r − λ)/λ into λ prime to p and λ = μp). Note m may be negative, so they also cover C(−mp^r − 1, k) and C(−mp^r, k). Congruence (iii) above is the reduction of (i) mod p^r.

**[quoted, p. 147] LEMMA 3.** "Let a_k ∈ ℤ_p (k = 0, 1, 2, 3, …) be such that Σ_{[kp^{−s}] = l} a_k ≡ 0 (mod p^s) for any s, l ∈ ℤ_{≥0}. Let e ∈ ℕ. Then
(i) Σ_{[kp^{−r}] = l} a_k C(mp^r − 1, k)² C(−mp^r − 1, k)^e (−1)^{ke} ≡ 0 (mod p^r) ∀ l, m ∈ ℤ_{≥0}. (7)
If, in addition a_k = 0 for all k ≡ 0 (mod p), then
(ii) Σ_{[kp^{−r}] = l} a_k C(mp^r − 1, k)² C(−mp^r − 1, k − 1)^e (−1)^{ke} ≡ 0 (mod p^r) ∀ l, m ∈ ℤ_{≥0}. (8)"
Proof: induction on r. Apply C(mp^r − 1, k) ≡ C(mp^{r−1} − 1, [k/p])(−1)^{{k/p}p} (mod p^r) (a consequence of (iii)); "Note that k + {k/p}p ≡ [k/p] (mod 2)"; collect terms with the same n = [k/p]; the new coefficients ā_n = p^{−1} Σ_{[k/p]=n} a_k are in ℤ_p and satisfy the same block hypothesis (Σ_{[np^{−s}]=l} ā_n = p^{−1} Σ_{[kp^{−s−1}]=l} a_k ≡ 0 mod p^s); the induction hypothesis at r − 1 then gives A ≡ p · (≡ 0 mod p^{r−1}) ≡ 0 mod p^r. For (ii): "Apply the first induction step to the left-hand side of (8) and notice that we may put [k − 1/p] = [k/p] since we sum essentially over (k, p) = 1. After this step we are back again in the situation of Lemma 3(i)." **[quoted, p. 148]**
This is the "block-sum induction"; Straub's Lemma 5.6 is its abstract form (any kernel C(p^r n; k) with C(p^r n; k) ≡ C(p^{r−1} n; [k/p]) mod p^r).

**[quoted, p. 148] LEMMA 4.** "Let s, t ∈ ℤ_{≥0}. Then
(i) Σ_{[np^{−t}] = i} ( Σ'_{[kp^{−s}] = n} 1/k ) ( Σ'_{λ=1}^{n} 1/λ ) ≡ 0 (mod p^{t+2s}) ∀ i ∈ ℤ. (9)
(ii) Σ'_{[kp^{−t}] = i} (1/k) Σ'_{λ=1}^{kp^s} 1/λ ≡ 0 (mod p^{t+s}) ∀ i ∈ ℤ."
Proof of (i), s ≥ 1 (pp. 148–149): write the inner block sum as Σ'_{κ=1}^{p^s − 1} 1/(κ + np^s) = Σ'_κ (1/κ − np^s/κ² + n²p^{2s}/κ³ − …) — **a full geometric expansion in np^s/κ, i.e. all orders, not just the first**. Since Σ' 1/κ ≡ 0 (mod p^{2s}), Σ' 1/κ² ≡ 0 (mod p^s), "etc.", it suffices to show Σ_{n=0}^{p^t − 1} n^k Σ'_{λ=1}^{n} 1/λ ≡ 0 (mod p^t) for all k ≥ 0, equivalently (10): Σ_{n=0}^{p^t − 1} k! C(n,k) (Σ'_{λ≤n} 1/λ) ≡ 0 (mod p^t). This is done by an Abel-summation identity: the sum equals k! C(p^t, k+1) Σ'_{λ<p^t} 1/λ − (k!/(k+1)) Σ_{n<p^t} C(n,k) ≡ −(k!/(k+1)²) p^t C(p^t − 1, k) (mod p^t), and "Since v_p((k+1)²) ≤ v_p(k!) if p ≥ 5, we see that the left-hand side of (10) is indeed zero mod p^t." **[quoted, p. 149]** (The reduction from the block [np^{−t}] = i to n ∈ [0, p^t) is stated without comment; **[inference]** it uses that Σ'_{λ≤n} 1/λ changes by full p^t-blocks, ≡ 0 mod p^{2t} by Lemma 1, plus a shift by ip^t inside the remaining partial block, harmless mod p^t after the p^{2s} from the outer coefficients.)
Proof of (ii) (p. 149): reflection λ ↦ p^{t+s} − λ gives Σ'_{λ=1}^{kp^s} 1/λ ≡ Σ'_{λ=1}^{(p^t − k)p^s} 1/λ (mod p^{t+s}), hence 2 Σ'_{k<p^t} (1/k) Σ'_{λ≤kp^s} 1/λ ≡ Σ'_{k<p^t} (1/k + 1/(p^t − k)) Σ'_{λ≤kp^s} 1/λ ≡ 0 (mod p^{t+s}) (the bracket is p^t/(k(p^t − k)), the inner sum is ≡ 0 mod p^{2s} by Lemma 1).
**[inference]** Lemma 4 is the "double harmonic sum" engine: (i) is Σ_blocks σ_s(n) H'_n ≡ 0 mod p^{t+2s} with σ_s(n) := Σ'_{[k/p^s]=n} 1/k the harmonic weight of a p^s-block, and (ii) is Σ' (1/k) H'_{kp^s} ≡ 0 mod p^{t+s}. At (s,t) = (0,1), (ii) is the classical Σ_{k<p} H_k/k ≡ 0 (mod p).

**[quoted, p. 150] LEMMA 5.** "For any l ∈ ℤ_{≥0}, m ∈ ℕ we have
Σ'_{[kp^{−r}] = l} (1/k) C(mp^r − 1, k)² C(−mp^r − 1, k − 1) (−1)^k ≡ 0 (mod p^{2r}). (11)"
**This is the lemma that handles the one-power-of-p situation.** Proof (pp. 150–151): define, for s = 0, 1, …, r − 1,
 A_s = Σ_{[np^{−s}] = l} ( Σ'_{[kp^{−r+s}] = n} 1/k ) C(mp^s − 1, n)² C(−mp^s − 1, n) (−1)^n,
i.e. the harmonic weight 1/k is pushed down r − s digits to the block weight σ_{r−s}(n), while the binomial "unit part" is evaluated at level s. Claim: A_s ≡ 0 (mod p^{2r}) for all s ≤ r − 1.
* s = 0: the binomials are constants C(m−1, l)² C(−m−1, l)(−1)^l and A_0 ≡ 0 mod p^{2r} "is clear by Lemma 1".
* s → s+1: by Lemma 2(i) (applied to all three binomials, with ±m),
 C(mp^{s+1} − 1, n)² C(−mp^{s+1} − 1, n)(−1)^n ≡ C(mp^s − 1, [n/p])² C(−mp^s − 1, [n/p]) (−1)^{[n/p]} Π'_{λ=1}^{n} (1 − mp^{s+1}/λ) ≡ (same) × (1 − Σ'_{λ=1}^{n} mp^{s+1}/λ) (mod p^{2s+2}). **[quoted formulas, p. 150]**
 **[inference, bookkeeping]** The three factors give Π'((mp^{s+1} − λ)/λ)²((−mp^{s+1} − λ)/λ) = ±Π'(1 − x_λ)²(1 + x_λ) = ±Π'(1 − x_λ − x_λ² + x_λ³) with x_λ = mp^{s+1}/λ; dropping the x_λ² terms and expanding the product to first order is legitimate mod p^{2s+2} = p^{2 v_p(x)}. This is where the "square × single factor" structure C(N−1,k)² C(−N−1,k) pays off: the first-order coefficient is exactly −Σ' x_λ = −mp^{s+1} H'_n, with no 1/λ² term at first order.
 Substituting and collecting terms with the same q = [n/p]:
 A_{s+1} ≡ A_s − mp^{s+1} Σ_{[qp^{−s}] = l} p^{2(r−s)−1} a_q C(mp^s − 1, q)² C(−mp^s − 1, q)(−1)^q (mod p^{2r}),
 with a_q = p^{−2(r−s)+1} Σ_{[n/p] = q} ( Σ'_{[kp^{−r+s+1}] = n} 1/k )( Σ'_{λ=1}^{n} 1/λ ). **[quoted, pp. 150–151]**
 (The truncation error is absorbed because the weight Σ'_{[kp^{−r+s+1}]=n} 1/k is ≡ 0 mod p^{2(r−s−1)} by Lemma 1, and 2(r−s−1) + 2s + 2 = 2r. **[inference]**)
 "Notice that by Lemma 4(i) for any j ∈ ℤ, t ∈ ℕ, Σ_{[qp^{−t}] = j} a_q = Σ_{[np^{−t−1}] = j} p^{−2(r−s)+1} (Σ' 1/k)(Σ' 1/λ) ≡ 0 (mod p^t), and thus we can apply Lemma 3 with a_q, s instead of a_q, r to obtain A_{s+1} − A_s ≡ 0 (mod p^{2r})." **[quoted, p. 151]** (Lemma 4(i) with (s, t) → (r−s−1, t+1) makes a_q p-integral and gives the block hypothesis; Lemma 3(i) with e = 1 at level s gives p^s; total exponent (s+1) + (2r − 2s − 1) + s = 2r. **[inference]**)
* Conclusion: A_{r−1} ≡ 0 (mod p^{2r}); "To conclude the proof of our lemma, we reduce the left-hand side of (11) in the same way as in our induction step, where in addition we replace [k − 1/p] by [k/p] since we sum over (k, p) = 1. By this step the left-hand side of (11) reduces to A_{r−1} (mod p^{2r}), which we know to be zero mod p^{2r}." **[quoted, p. 151]**
 **[inference — a terse spot]** In this last step the third binomial has index k − 1, so Lemma 2(i) produces Π'_{λ≤k−1} instead of Π'_{λ≤k}, i.e. an extra factor k/(−mp^r − k) ≡ −(1 − mp^r/k) (mod p^{2r}); the first-order coefficient at the top level is therefore H'_k + 1/k, not H'_k. The extra term is −mp^r Σ'_{block} (1/k²) C(mp^{r−1} − 1, [k/p])² C(−mp^{r−1} − 1, [k/p])(±1), and it is ≡ 0 mod p^{2r} by Lemma 3(i) at level r − 1 with coefficients p^{−1} Σ'_{[k/p]=n} 1/k² (block sums Σ'_{[k/p^{s+1}]=j} 1/k² ≡ 0 mod p^{s+1}) — the same mechanism as in the u-proof below. Beukers does not spell this out; the text says only "in the same way".

## 3. Proof of Theorem 1, ζ(3) case u_n (pp. 151–152) — two explicit powers of p

Split u_{mp^r − 1} = Σ_{l=0}^{mp^{r−1}−1} C(mp^r − 1, lp)² C(−mp^r, lp)² + Σ'_{k} C(mp^r − 1, k)² C(−mp^r, k)² (using C(n+k,k)² = C(−n−1,k)², here with n = mp^r − 1).
* **Unscaled terms (p ∤ k).** "The second summation on the right-hand side equals Σ'_{k=0}^{mp^r − 1} (m²p^{2r}/k²) C(mp^r − 1, k)² C(−mp^r − 1, k − 1)². (12) Since Σ'_{[kp^{−s}] = n} 1/k² ≡ 0 (mod p^s) ∀ n ∈ ℤ, s ∈ ℕ, we can apply Lemma 3(ii) with a_k = k^{−2} if p ∤ k, a_k = 0 otherwise, and conclude that expression (12) is zero mod p^{3r}." **[quoted, p. 151]**
 Bookkeeping: p^{2r} explicit (two factors C(−mp^r, k) = −(mp^r/k) C(−mp^r − 1, k − 1)) × p^r from Lemma 3(ii) with e = 2. Only Σ 1/k² block sums are needed; no first-order harmonic sums, no expansion of products.
* **Scaled terms (k = lp).** Lemma 2 (exact) gives u_{mp^r − 1} ≡ Σ_l C(mp^{r−1} − 1, l)² C(−mp^{r−1}, l)² Π'_{λ=1}^{lp} (1 − m²p^{2r}/λ²)² (mod p^{3r}), then "Π'_{λ=1}^{lp} (1 − m²p^{2r}/λ²)² ≡ 1 − 2m²p^{2r} Σ'_{λ=1}^{lp} 1/λ² (mod p^{3r}) ≡ 1 (mod p^{min(3r, 2r + v_p(lp))})" and "C(−mp^{r−1}, l)² = m² (p^{2r−2}/l²) C(−mp^{r−1} − 1, l − 1)² ≡ 0 (mod p^{2 max(r − v_p(lp), 0)})" **[quoted, p. 152]**; hence termwise matching mod p^{3r} (exponent (2r + v) + 2(r − v) = 4r − v ≥ 3r for v ≤ r; for v ≥ r the Σ' 1/λ² over full p^r-blocks already gives 3r).

## 4. Proof of Theorem 1, ζ(2) case a_n (pp. 152–154) — ONE explicit power of p

Split a_{mp^r − 1} = Σ_{l=0}^{mp^{r−1}−1} C(mp^r − 1, lp)² C(−mp^r, lp)(−1)^{lp} + Σ'_k C(mp^r − 1, k)² C(−mp^r, k)(−1)^k.

**(a) Unscaled terms (p ∤ k): one explicit p^r, two more from Lemma 5.**
"Writing C(−mp^r, k) = −(mp^r/k) C(−mp^r − 1, k − 1) we see the second summation is zero mod p^{3r} according to Lemma 5." **[quoted, p. 152]**
Bookkeeping: p^r explicit × p^{2r} from Lemma 5 (the harmonic-weighted block sum Σ'_{block} (1/k) × unit part ≡ 0 mod p^{2r}). This is the whole answer to "how are the terms with only one factor p^r shown to contribute 0 mod p^{3r}": **not** by extracting more powers from binomials (none are available: C(mp^r − 1, k) is a p-adic unit for k < p, and ≡ ±C(mp^{r−1} − 1, [k/p]) in general), but by a **cancellation of the weighted block sum two p-adic orders deeper than the trivial one**. The ingredients of that cancellation, in order of appearance in the induction of Lemma 5: Lemma 1 (Σ' 1/λ over a p^r-block ≡ 0 mod p^{2r}, i.e. Wolstenholme in block form) as the base case; Lemma 2(i) (exact product) + first-order expansion in mp^{s+1}/λ with the harmonic sum H'_n as the first-order coefficient; Lemma 4(i) (double harmonic block sums ≡ 0 mod p^{t+2s}) to control the correction; Lemma 3(i) (block-sum induction) to finish each step.

**(b) Scaled terms (k = lp): they do NOT match termwise; the first-order harmonic term must be summed out.**
Lemma 2 gives a_{mp^r − 1} ≡ Σ_l C(mp^{r−1} − 1, l)² C(−mp^{r−1}, l)(−1)^l Π'_{λ=1}^{lp} (1 − m²p^{2r}/λ²)((mp^r − λ)/λ) (mod p^{3r}). As in the u-proof, the factor Π'(1 − m²p^{2r}/λ²) ≡ 1 (mod p^{min(3r, 2r + v_p(lp))}) can be dropped against C(−mp^{r−1}, l) ≡ 0 (mod p^{max(r − v_p(lp), 0)}). What remains is
 a_{mp^r − 1} ≡ Σ_l C(mp^{r−1} − 1, l)² C(−mp^{r−1}, l)(−1)^l Π'_{λ=1}^{lp} (1 − mp^r/λ) (mod p^{3r}),
 "Observe, Π'_{λ=1}^{lp} (1 − mp^r/λ) ≡ 1 − mp^r Σ'_{λ=1}^{lp} 1/λ (mod p^{2r + v_p(lp)}). And thus
 a_{mp^r − 1} ≡ a_{mp^{r−1} − 1} − mp^r Σ_{l=0}^{mp^{r−1}−1} ( Σ'_{λ=1}^{lp} 1/λ ) C(mp^{r−1} − 1, l)² C(−mp^{r−1}, l)(−1)^l (mod p^{3r}).
 It suffices to show that the summation on the right-hand side is zero mod p^{2r} and then we are done." **[quoted, p. 153]**
 **[inference — where "second order" is controlled]** The first-order truncation mod p^{2r + v} (v = v_p(lp)) is justified because the second-order term m²p^{2r} e_2(1/λ) = m²p^{2r}((Σ' 1/λ)² − Σ' 1/λ²)/2 has Σ'_{λ≤lp} 1/λ ≡ 0 mod p^{2v} (Lemma 1: lp is a union of p^v-blocks) and Σ'_{λ≤lp} 1/λ² ≡ 0 mod p^v; so second order is O(p^{2r+v}), which the factor C(−mp^{r−1}, l) ≡ 0 mod p^{r−v} lifts to p^{3r}. Beukers never expands beyond first order *here*; the only all-orders expansion is inside Lemma 4(i).
 The residual sum is treated by **stratifying by s = v_p(lp)** (p. 153): for v_p(lp) ≥ r, Σ'_{λ≤lp} 1/λ ≡ 0 (mod p^{2r}) by Lemma 1 and those l contribute nothing. For fixed s ∈ {1, …, r−1}, write l = kp^{s−1} with p ∤ k; the stratum is
 Σ'_{k=0}^{mp^{r−s}−1} ( Σ'_{λ=1}^{kp^s} 1/λ ) C(mp^{r−1} − 1, kp^{s−1})² C(−mp^{r−1}, kp^{s−1}) (−1)^{kp^{s−1}} (13),
 to be shown ≡ 0 (mod p^{2r}). "Notice that p^{r−s} | C(−mp^{r−1}, kp^{s−1}) and Σ'_1^{kp^s} 1/λ ≡ 0 (mod p^{2s}). ## 6. Straub 2014 (arXiv:1401.0854): the other route to the ζ(2) shifted case

Straub's Theorem 3.2(b): for λ = (λ_1, …, λ_ℓ) with ℓ ≥ 2 and all λ_j ≤ 2, the multivariate sums A_{λ,ε}(n) = Σ_k ε^k Π_j multinomial(…) (eq. (20)) satisfy A_{λ,ε}(p^r n) ≡ A_{λ,ε}(p^{r−1} n) (mod p^{3r}) for p ≥ 5 **and all n ∈ ℤ^d, negative entries included** (binomials with negative entries defined by the Γ-limit; negation rule (11): C(n,k) = sgn(k)(−1)^k C(−n+k−1, k)).
* Apéry numbers: λ = (2,2); Remark 1.3 shows A(−n) = A(n−1), so (3) is (2) with −m. "The proof is an adaption of F. Beukers' proof [Beu85]" (p. 2, about Coster).
* ζ(2) numbers B(n) = Σ C(n,k)² C(n+k,k): λ = (2,1) (Example 3.4), B(n_1,n_2,n_3) = Σ_k C(n_1,k) C(n_1+n_2−k, n_1) C(n_3,k). "Proceeding as in Remark 1.3, and using the curious identity Σ_{k=0}^{n} C(n,k)² C(n+k,k) = Σ_{k=0}^{n} (−1)^{n+k} C(n,k) C(n+k,k)² (26), we find that B(−n) = (−1)^{n−1} B(n−1) for n > 0. Consequently, (25) implies the shifted supercongruences B(p^r m − 1) ≡ B(p^{r−1} m − 1), which hold modulo p^{3r} for all primes p ≥ 5 and were first proved in [Beu85], along with (3). We observe that, among the known Apéry-like numbers, the sequence B(n) and the Apéry numbers (1) are the only ones to satisfy shifted supercongruences of the form (3) in addition to the supercongruences of the form (2)." **[quoted, p. 8]** (I checked (26) at n = 1, 2: 3 = −1 + 4, 19 = 1 − 18 + 36.)
* How Straub's proof handles the unscaled terms (§5, proof of Theorem 3.2, pp. 16–17): with ℓ = 2 every summand A_λ(p^r n; k) contains two factors of the form C(p^r n_i, k), each ≡ 0 mod p^r for p ∤ k (for negative n_i this factor *is* C(−p^r m, k) = (−1)^k C(p^r m + k − 1, k), the "one-power" factor); hence G_0(p^r n) = p^{2r} n_1 n_{1+λ_1} Σ_{p∤k} (ε^k/k²) C(p^r n; k − 1) with integral C, and Lemma 5.6 (= Beukers Lemma 3) with a_k = ε^k/k² (Lemma 5.2: Σ'_{k<p^r} ε^k/k² ≡ 0 mod p^r) gives the third p^r (eq. (47)). Scaled terms: Lemma 5.3, termwise, from Jacobsthal's congruence (Lemma 5.1, Gessel 1983 / Granville 1997) in quotient form, C(p^r m_1, p^s k)/C(p^{r−1} m_1, p^{s−1} k) ≡ 1 mod p^{r+s+min(r,s)}, plus p^{2(r−s)} | A_λ(p^r n; p^s k) for s ≤ r.
* **Contrast [inference]:** Straub's proof contains **no first-order harmonic sums at all** (no analogue of Beukers's Lemmas 1, 4, 5) and no product expansions; the ζ(2) one-power problem is dissolved *before* the p-adic analysis by the transformation identity (26), which moves the square onto the C(n+k,k) factor so that the negative-index sum has two "C(N+k−1,k)-type" factors. Beukers's 1985 proof is the direct route that needs no such identity. Both are valid; Straub's is shorter but requires the sequence to be in his family A_{λ,ε} (or to admit a (26)-type dual identity).

Related secondary statements: Sun (arXiv:2409.06544, p. 1) restates Theorem 1 as "In [B1] Beukers showed that for any prime p > 3 and m, r ∈ ℤ⁺, A_{mp^r−1} ≡ A_{mp^{r−1}−1} (mod p^{3r}), A'_{mp^r−1} ≡ A'_{mp^{r−1}−1} (mod p^{3r})" (A = ζ(3), A' = ζ(2) numbers) and then works on different congruences. Osburn–Sahu–Straub (arXiv:1312.2195) treat only unshifted sequences; their Lemma 2.4 is Beukers's congruence (iii) ("Following [4, Lemma 2], we split the defining product…") and their Lemmas 2.2–2.5 + induction (35)–(36) are the Lemma 3 / Σ1/k² mechanism with half-block splits.

## 7. Delaygue 2018 and Malik–Straub 2016 (task 3)

**Delaygue, arXiv:1310.4131v2 (Compositio Math. 154 (2018)).** No theorem of the form a(mp^r − 1) ≡ a(mp^{r−1} − 1) (mod p^{3r}) occurs; the word "supercongruence" does not occur. The paper proves p-adic valuation lower bounds A(n) ∈ p^{α} ℤ_p with α = #{digits n_i of n with A(n_i) ≡ 0 mod p} (Theorem 1 for multisums of factorial ratios S_{e,f} with 2-admissible e; Theorem 2 for constant terms of powers of Laurent polynomials with reflexive Newton polytope) — this settles Beukers's Conjectures A–C, which come from Beukers's 1986/87 Groupe de travail note ([3] there), *not* from the 1985 paper ([4] there, cited only in the list "In the 1980's, several congruences satisfied by those sequences were demonstrated (see for example [4], [5], [10], [16], [22])", §1.2). Method: p-Lucas property (from Samol–van Straten / Mellit–Vlasenko, or from Proposition 2 for S_{e,f}) + an induction on valuations driven by the Apéry-like recurrence (Lemma 1, §3.1, with differential operators of "type I/II"); the shifted index −1 + mp appears only inside that induction (type II: P_2(X) ∈ (X+1)ℤ_p[X], so P_2(−1+mp)A(−1+mp) ∈ p·A(p−1+(m−1)p)ℤ_p). ## 8. Applicability to the target (A376462 shifted) — my assessment **[inference throughout]**

Target: a(N−1) = Σ_{k,j} S̃(N;k,j), S̃ = C(N−1,k)² C(N+k−1,k) C(N−1,k+j)² C(N+j−1,j), N = mp^r; classes K (p∤k, p|j) and J (p|k, p∤j) carry one explicit p^r, class B (p∤k, p∤j) two, class S none.

1. **The one-power situation is exactly Beukers's ζ(2) case, and he solved it without any dual identity.** The project roadmap's sentence "the Apéry numbers escape this through Straub's identity (26)" describes Straub's 2014 route only. In 1985 the ζ(2) shifted congruence was proved directly: the explicit p^r from C(−N,k) = −(N/k)C(−N−1,k−1) is supplemented by Lemma 5, a *mod p^{2r}* cancellation of the harmonic-weighted block sum Σ'_{p^r-block}(1/k)·U(N;k), U = unit part. So the absence of a (26)-analogue for A376462 (`a376462_dual_identity.txt`) is not an obstruction in principle; it only rules out the short route.

2. **Dictionary with the project's class-K dissection (roadmap §A).** Beukers's A_{r−1} ≡ 0 (mod p^{2r}) is, at r = 2, Σ'_{[k/p²]=l} (1/k) U(mp; [k/p]) ≡ 0 (mod p⁴) — the same shape as the project's "T0 := Σ'_k Ψ(M,[k/p])/k ≡ 0 (mod p⁴) blockwise over p²-blocks" with Ψ(M,·) in the role of U(mp;·). Beukers's induction step reduces it to A_{r−2} (Lemma 1 at the base) plus Lemma 4(i) at s = 1: Σ_{[n/p^t]=i} σ(n) H'_n ≡ 0 (mod p^{t+2}) with σ(n) = Σ'_{[k/p]=n} 1/k. Since σ(n) ≡ p²(h_1 − n h_2) (mod p³) with h_1 = −B_{p−3}/3, h_2 = 2B_{p−3}/3, the t = 1 case of Lemma 4(i) *is* the project's moment identity Σ_{w<p}(1 + 2w)H_w ≡ 0 (mod p) — but Beukers's proof of Lemma 4(i) (geometric expansion in np^s/κ + the Abel-summation identity (10)) is Bernoulli-free and valid for all s, t at once, i.e. it is the general-r version the roadmap says is still needed ("For general r the same structure repeats level by level"). Likewise the r = 1 proof already in `notes/A376462-proof-first-congruence.md` §5 uses Σ'1/k ≡ 0 (mod p²) and Σ' H'_k/k ≡ 0 (mod p), which are Beukers's Lemma 1 (r = 1) and Lemma 4(ii) ((s,t) = (0,1)); Lemma 5 is the lift of that argument to r ≥ 2, and Lemma 3 is the bookkeeping that makes the lift inductive.

3. **What must be re-proved for the target, and what is new.** Beukers's induction needs, for the kernel in use, (a) the exact product reduction level by level (Lemma 2(i) applies verbatim to each of the six binomial factors of S̃, with ±N), (b) a first-order model U(mp^{s+1}; n) ≡ U(mp^s; [n/p])·(1 − mp^{s+1} ρ(n)) (mod p^{2s+2}) with an explicit first-order coefficient ρ, and (c) the Lemma 4(i)-type congruence Σ_{[n/p^t]=i} σ_s(n) ρ(n) ≡ 0 (mod p^{t+2s}) for that ρ (and Lemma 4(ii)-type for the scaled class). For Beukers ρ(n) = H'_n (plus 1/n at the top level, see the terse spot in §2). For S̃ in class K (p ∤ k, p | j) the same expansion of the six factors — C(N−1,k)² → Π'_{λ≤k}(1 − N/λ)², C(N+k−1,k) = (N/k)·Π_{λ≤k−1}(N+λ)/λ → (N/k)·Π'_{λ≤k−1}(1 + N/λ)·(p-part), C(N−1,k+j)² → Π'_{λ≤k+j}(1 − N/λ)², C(N+j−1,j) → Π'_{λ≤j}(1 + N/λ)·(p-part) — gives the exact first-order coefficient ρ(k,j) = 2H'_k − H'_{k−1} + 2H'_{k+j} − H'_j = H'_k + 1/k + 2H'_{k+j} − H'_j. Writing H'_{k+j} = H'_j + Σ'_{λ≤k} 1/(j+λ) this is ρ = 3H'_k + 1/k + H'_j + 2(Σ'_{λ≤k} 1/(j+λ) − H'_k), where H'_j ≡ 0 (mod p^{2v_p(j)}) by Lemma 1 and the last bracket is ≡ −2j Σ'_{λ≤k} 1/λ² (mod p^{2v_p(j)}); the leading part 3H'_k + 1/k agrees with the project's machine-checked model B(k) ≡ Ψ(M,[k/p])(1 − N(3H'_k + 1/k)) (mod p⁴ at r = 2), and the remaining pieces are the 'j-coupled' corrections. The H'-part of ρ is covered by 3 × Lemma 4(i); the 1/k part by the Σ'1/k² block sums (Lemma 3(ii) with a_k = 1/k², as in the u-proof). **What Beukers does not provide** is the Lemma 4(i)-analogue for the *j-coupled* part of Ψ: the inner j-sum makes the "unit part" at level s depend on the j-digits as well, and the roadmap's open item ("the non-harmonic part of ρ coming from the j'-sum … and the same moment identity for it") is precisely a Lemma 4(i) for a kernel that is not a pure harmonic sum. Beukers's proof technique for Lemma 4(i) (expand 1/(κ + np^s) to all orders, then prove Σ_{n<p^t} n^k · (kernel)(n) ≡ 0 mod p^t for every k by an exact summation identity) is the natural template: one needs the moments Σ_{n<p^t} n^k ρ_j(n) ≡ 0 (mod p^t) of the non-harmonic kernel.

4. **Class S behaves as in Beukers, not as in Straub.** The observation that scaled pairs "match only in aggregate (termwise deficit −1 at r = 2)" is exactly the situation on p. 153 of Beukers: the surviving first-order term −mp^r Σ'_{λ≤lp} 1/λ is not termwise zero mod p^{3r}; Beukers kills it by stratifying over v_p(lp) = s, reducing the binomials to level r − s mod p^{r−s} / p^{2r−2s}, and applying Lemma 3(ii) with the weights (p^{−s}/k) H'_{kp^s} (hypothesis by Lemma 4(ii)). For S̃ the corresponding first-order term in class S is −N·(harmonic combination in k, j, k+j); the same stratification (now by min or by the pair (v_p(kp), v_p(jp))) and a two-index Lemma 3 (its proof is index-agnostic: only the "C(p^r n; k, j) ≡ C(p^{r−1} n; [k/p], [j/p]) mod p^r" property and block hypotheses on the weights are used) would be the direct generalisation. Jacobsthal/Kazandzidis refinements (the roadmap's "Jacobsthal–Zhao with B_{p−3}") are not needed in Beukers's route at all — he never uses quotient congruences for binomials; everything is done with exact products and harmonic block sums.

5. **Caveats.** (i) Beukers's method is a genuine two-level induction (in r for Lemma 3, in s for Lemma 5); the paper calls it "brute force". For a double sum the bookkeeping doubles, but no step is conceptually new except item 3's non-harmonic Lemma 4(i). (ii) p ≥ 5 is used in Lemma 1, Lemma 4(i) (v_p((k+1)²) ≤ v_p(k!)) and the Σ'1/λ² congruence; the project's p = 3 side observation would need separate treatment. (iii) I have not attempted to carry out the generalisation; the statements in items 3–4 about S̃ are sketches derived from Beukers's formulas, not verified computations.
